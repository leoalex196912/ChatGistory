================================================================================
KEY ENGINEERING INSIGHTS PRESERVED
================================================================================

1. Spring/slot geometric coupling:
   SLOT_DEPTH <= SPRING_GROOVE_DEPTH + 1.80mm
   This is the hard constraint that prevents the spring from losing contact
   with the needle outer face. Future iterations MUST respect this.

2. Cam ring vs cylinder rotation:
   CYLINDER rotates, CAM RING is stationary.
   This is non-negotiable for any CSM matching Erlbacher architecture.
   Yarn feeders are on the stationary frame.

3. Slot depth is mechanical, not visual:
   Photos exaggerate depth via shadows. The needle stem only needs 1.80mm.
   Anything beyond that is dynamic-clearance margin. PETG can't tolerate
   the same depths that machined aluminum cylinders use.

4. Root fillets at slot bottoms are MANDATORY for PETG:
   Sharp 90 deg internal corners along layer lines = fatigue crack starters
   under cyclic cam loading. SLOT_ROOT_FILLET = 0.5mm is the minimum.

5. The wedge test is the only way to validate:
   Static geometry calculations can't predict fin flex under dynamic load.
   The wedge has 6 slots varying both width and depth specifically so the
   physical test answers the question that math cannot.

6. PETG is for prototypes only:
   Material progression for production: PETG -> PA12-CF/PPA-CF -> machined Al.
   Accept creep as a known limitation; iterate the design fast on PETG.

================================================================================
OPEN QUESTIONS / FUTURE V4 IDEAS
================================================================================

1. Profiled slot floor:
   Real industrial cylinders have non-rectangular slot interiors -- shallow
   waist with deeper relief in the middle to match real needle contact
   patterns (needle contacts at upper guide + lower butt, not continuously).
   Major change to cutter geometry; defer to V4 if wedge shows binding.

2. Dynamic fin flex under cyclic loading:
   Static hand-pressure tests are necessary but insufficient. Real failure
   modes (harmonic vibration, butt impacts, creep) only show up after
   50k-1M cycles. Plan for replacement cycles even with perfect prototype.

3. Top dome / fin top profile:
   If FIN_TOP_CHAMFER = 0.6mm is still not visually sufficient, can push
   to 0.8mm (constraint: fin width at top = 1.99mm, chamfer per side max
   0.99mm). Or consider rounded loft profile for a true filleted look.

4. Sinker ring interfacing:
   Sinker Ring V1.0 has 72 sinkers projecting inward; how they interact
   with the 0.6mm fin top chamfers needs verification when both parts are
   in hand.
