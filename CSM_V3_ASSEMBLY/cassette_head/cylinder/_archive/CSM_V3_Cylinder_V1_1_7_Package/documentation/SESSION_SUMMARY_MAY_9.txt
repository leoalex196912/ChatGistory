## Phase 4: User Identified Spring Position Wrong

User observed: real Erlbacher cylinders have spring at TOP, not bottom.
Provided authoritative reference about Erlbacher Knitting Machines:

  "The spring is located at the TOP of the cylinder"
  "Sits in horizontal groove just below needle hooks, above cams"  
  "Spring sits approximately halfway between needle latch and top of cylinder"
  "Function: tension band, presses needles firmly against back of slots"
  "Holds needles upright, prevents them from falling out"

## Phase 5: V1.1.6 - Spring Moved to Top

Created V1.1.6 with:
- Spring groove relocated from Z=15.5-18.7 (bottom) to Z=66.4-69.6 (top)
- Spring center Z=68mm (calculated halfway between latch and cylinder top)
- Bottom groove DELETED
- Both production macro and viz macro updated

## Phase 6: Engineering Review of V1.1.6

Reviewer identified 5 problems with V1.1.6:

PROBLEM 1: Spring too high (Z=68 only 7mm below top)
  - Limited material above groove (top edge fragile)
  - Concerns about hook/latch clearance
  - RECOMMENDATION: Z=63.5 or Z=62

PROBLEM 2: Spring protrusion too large (1.59mm past OD)
  - May snag yarn carrier
  - Real machines recess spring more
  - RECOMMENDATION: Groove depth 1.8mm (protrusion 0.99mm)

PROBLEM 3: Needle radial position oversimplified
  - Currently centered in slot depth
  - Reality: spring tension presses needle against BACK wall
  - RECOMMENDATION: NEEDLE_RADIAL_CENTER = CYL_OUTER_R - SLOT_DEPTH + STEM_D/2 + 0.05

PROBLEM 4: Spring is ideal torus (no split/clasp)
  - DEFERRED - reviewer agreed this is acceptable for visualization

PROBLEM 5: Slot geometry vertical-only (no fillets/sweeps)
  - DEFERRED - reviewer agreed this is acceptable for prototype

## Phase 7: V1.1.7 - All 3 Critical Fixes Applied

User chose:
- Z=62mm (most conservative, reviewer's second option)
- Depth 1.8mm (reviewer's recommendation)
- Apply needle position fix

V1.1.7 final geometry:
- Spring groove: Z=60.4-63.6mm (center Z=62)
- Groove depth: 1.8mm
- Spring protrusion past OD: 0.99mm
- Material above groove: 11.4mm (was 5.4mm in V1.1.6)
- Needle radial center: 56.10mm (against back wall)
- Spring overlap with stem: 1.14mm (proper tension contact)

================================================================================
KEY DESIGN INSIGHTS LEARNED
================================================================================

1. CYLINDER SPRING PURPOSE
   The spring is a TENSION BAND around the OD that presses needle stems
   against the back wall of slots. It's NOT a cam return mechanism.
   
2. SPRING vs CAM ROLES (separate functions):
   - SPRING engages STEM near top → retention, holds needles in place
   - CAM RING engages BUTT L-bend at Z=19-23 → lift mechanism
   
3. WHY SPRING NEAR TOP:
   - Long thin stems flex outward without retention
   - Spring at top gives maximum lever arm for stabilization
   - Like a beam support: hold it where the deflection is largest
   
4. WHY NOT AT BOTTOM:
   - Butt and L-bend are inherently constrained by butt cavity walls
   - Bottom spring provides minimal stem retention
   - Original V1.1.4-V1.1.5 design was fundamentally wrong

5. AUTHORITATIVE REFERENCE: Erlbacher Knitting Machines
   "Spring sits halfway between needle latch and top of cylinder"
   "Just below needle hooks, above cams"
   "Tension band pressing needles against back of slots"
